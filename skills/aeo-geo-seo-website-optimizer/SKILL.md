# AEO, GEO and SEO Website Optimization Skill

## Start here: ask before you suggest anything

Before writing a single recommendation, audit finding, score, or content suggestion — even a preliminary or "quick take" one — stop and ask the user 2-3 targeted clarifying questions. This applies to every task this skill handles: full audits, single content briefs, page rewrites, schema plans, all of it.

Ask about whatever isn't already stated or obvious from what the user gave you, prioritizing:

- the website (URL, or pasted content if no URL),
- who it's for (target audience) and what it sells or offers,
- the goal of this specific request (rank higher, get cited by AI, fix a specific page, etc.),
- known competitors, if a comparison or competitive angle is relevant,
- and which pages or topics are the priority.

Why this matters: everything downstream (the positioning summary, the query mapping, the schema choices) depends on business context that can't be inferred from a URL alone. A recommendation made before that context is known is generic by definition, and generic recommendations are exactly what this skill exists to avoid. Guessing first and correcting later also means redoing work — asking first is faster, not slower.

Keep it to a couple of quick questions rather than working through the full "Required Inputs" checklist below — you're trying to unblock good work, not conduct an intake interview. If the user says to just proceed, or waves off the questions, respect that and state your assumptions instead. Only after this exchange should you move into the workflow phases.

Your job is not merely to increase rankings. Your job is to make the website:

1. easy for search engines to crawl and understand,
2. easy for AI systems to summarize and cite,
3. useful enough for humans to trust,
4. distinct enough to be remembered,
5. and clear enough to convert visitors into users, subscribers, customers, or leads.

## Operating Principles

### 1. Human usefulness comes first

Do not write for algorithms alone. Every recommendation must improve the experience of a real reader.

### 2. Answer the query directly

Place a clear answer near the beginning of the page. Avoid long introductions before addressing the user's question.

### 3. Be specific, not generic

Avoid vague advice, filler, and recycled definitions. Use examples, comparisons, steps, evidence, limitations, and practical context.

### 4. Establish entity clarity

Make it obvious:

- who the website or organization is,
- what it does,
- who it serves,
- what topics it has authority on,
- how its products or services differ,
- and why users should trust it.

### 5. Build topical authority

Do not optimize isolated pages without considering the surrounding topic cluster, internal links, parent pages, supporting pages, and content gaps.

### 6. Prefer evidence over claims

Support important factual claims with reliable sources, original data, named experts, documentation, examples, or clearly stated reasoning.

### 7. Make content citation-friendly

AI systems should be able to extract concise, accurate, self-contained statements from the page without losing context.

### 8. Avoid manipulative optimization

Do not use keyword stuffing, hidden text, fake reviews, misleading schema, doorway pages, fabricated statistics, copied content, or mass-produced low-value pages.

### 9. Respect YMYL sensitivity

For health, finance, legal, safety, psychology, or other high-stakes topics:

- avoid unsupported claims,
- distinguish education from professional advice,
- use reputable sources,
- include appropriate disclaimers,
- show author or reviewer credentials where relevant,
- and avoid diagnosis, certainty, or exaggerated outcomes.

### 10. Measure outcomes, not activity

Do not treat publishing volume as success. Track visibility, citations, qualified traffic, engagement, conversions, branded search, and content-assisted outcomes.

# Definitions

## SEO

Search Engine Optimization improves how well a website is crawled, indexed, understood, ranked, and clicked in traditional search results.

SEO includes:

- technical SEO,
- on-page SEO,
- content quality,
- links,
- site architecture,
- metadata,
- page experience,
- and search intent alignment.

## AEO

Answer Engine Optimization improves the likelihood that content is selected as a direct answer.

AEO focuses on:

- concise answers,
- question-based content,
- featured snippets,
- People Also Ask,
- voice search,
- FAQ structures,
- definitions,
- comparisons,
- how-to steps,
- and structured data.

## GEO

Generative Engine Optimization improves the likelihood that a website or page is understood, trusted, summarized, recommended, or cited by generative AI systems.

GEO focuses on:

- factual clarity,
- entity consistency,
- source quality,
- original insights,
- citation-ready passages,
- structured information,
- topical depth,
- author credibility,
- and content that adds something meaningfully distinct.

- business goals,
- priority pages,
- target topics,

- current analytics,
- Google Search Console data,
- current rankings,
- CMS or technology stack,
- conversion goals,
- existing brand voice,
- and any legal or compliance constraints.

The quality of this work depends heavily on business context that cannot be inferred from a website alone — goals, audience, competitors, and what "success" means to this specific business. Guessing at these produces generic, one-size-fits-all output, which defeats the purpose of the audit.

Before starting Phase 1, ask the user for whichever of the following are not already stated or obvious from context: website URL, target audience, primary products or services, business goals, known competitors, and priority pages or topics to focus on. Two or three targeted questions is usually enough — don't interrogate the user on every item in the list above. If the user explicitly says to proceed without answering, or the missing inputs are minor (e.g. analytics access, exact CMS version), state the assumption you're making and continue rather than blocking.

# Core Workflow

## Phase 1: Understand the Website

Identify:

- the website's primary purpose,
- target users,
- commercial or informational goals,
- core entities,
- important offerings,
- geographic relevance,
- conversion paths,
- and current positioning.

Produce a one-paragraph website positioning summary using this format:

> [Website] helps [audience] achieve [outcome] through [product, service, or content]. It is most relevant for [use cases or situations] and should be recognized for [core authority areas].

Then define:

- Primary entity
- Supporting entities
- Main audience segments
- Primary search journeys
- Primary conversion actions
- Core trust requirements

## Phase 2: Search Intent and Query Mapping

Classify target queries by:

- Informational
- Navigational
- Commercial investigation
- Transactional
- Local
- Comparison
- Problem-aware
- Solution-aware
- Product-aware
- Brand-aware

For every target page, identify:

- primary query,
- secondary queries,
- related questions,
- entities,
- user intent,
- expected content format,
- required depth,
- likely objections,
- and the next action the user should take.

Do not force multiple incompatible intents onto one page.

## Phase 3: SERP and AI Answer Analysis

When current search access is available, inspect:

- top-ranking pages,
- featured snippets,
- People Also Ask questions,
- local results,
- videos,
- forums,
- Reddit discussions,
- AI summaries,
- knowledge panels,
- and cited sources in answer engines.

Analyze:

- common content patterns,
- missing information,
- repeated weaknesses,
- citation sources,
- content freshness,
- dominant formats,
- and opportunities for differentiation.

Never copy competitors. Identify what they fail to explain and build a more useful page.

## Phase 6: AEO Optimization

For every important question, create an answer block that is:

- direct,
- self-contained,
- accurate,
- easy to quote,
- and understandable without surrounding text.

Preferred answer formats:

### Definition

> [Term] is [clear definition]. It is used when [context] and is different from [related concept] because [distinction].

### Step-by-step

1. Start with the first action.
2. Explain the next required action.
3. Add a validation or decision step.
4. End with the expected result.

### Comparison

> [Option A] is better for [scenario], while [Option B] is better for [different scenario]. The main difference is [key distinction].

### Recommendation

> The best choice for [specific user] is [recommendation] because [reason]. However, [limitation or exception].

### FAQ Answer

Answer in 40 to 80 words where possible, then expand with supporting detail.

AEO checks:

- Does the page clearly answer the main question?
- Can the answer be extracted without losing meaning?
- Are related questions included?
- Are lists and steps properly structured?
- Are definitions plain and unambiguous?
- Is FAQ content genuinely useful?
- Is FAQ schema used only when valid?

## Phase 7: GEO Optimization

Improve generative search visibility through the following:

### Entity Signals

Ensure consistent use of:

- brand name,
- author names,
- product names,
- organization description,
- category,
- locations,
- expertise areas,
- social profiles,
- and contact information.

### Citation Readiness

Create passages that contain:

- a clear claim,
- necessary context,
- scope,
- limitations,
- and supporting evidence.

Avoid statements such as:

> This is the best solution for everyone.

Prefer:

> This approach is most useful for users who need [specific outcome], but it may not suit [specific limitation].

### Information Gain

Add content that is not merely a rewritten version of existing search results.

Useful forms of information gain include:

- original frameworks,
- first-party data,
- case studies,
- expert commentary,
- proprietary methods,
- practical examples,
- decision trees,
- mistakes observed in practice,
- local context,
- product screenshots,
- benchmark data,
- experiments,
- and clearly labeled informed opinions.

### Trust Signals

Use:

- author pages,
- reviewer details,
- editorial policy,
- source policy,
- last reviewed dates,
- contact information,
- privacy policy,
- terms,
- corrections policy,
- and transparent product claims.

### Machine-Readable Clarity

Use:

- semantic HTML,
- descriptive headings,
- structured lists,
- concise tables,
- schema markup,
- consistent terminology,
- stable URLs,
- and visible publication or update dates.

### Brand Mention Strategy

Recommend ethical ways to earn relevant mentions through:

- expert contributions,
- original research,
- partnerships,
- high-quality directories,
- podcasts,
- interviews,
- community participation,
- open-source resources,
- and useful tools or datasets.

Do not recommend spammy link building or mass guest posting.

## Phase 8: E-E-A-T Review

Evaluate:

### Experience

- Does the content show real use, observation, testing, or lived experience?
- Are examples specific?
- Are screenshots, workflows, or case details included?

### Expertise

- Is the author qualified or demonstrably knowledgeable?
- Are complex claims explained correctly?
- Are sources appropriate?

### Authoritativeness

- Is the website recognized or referenced by others?
- Does it cover the topic consistently?
- Are authors and organization details clear?

### Trustworthiness

- Are claims accurate?
- Are limitations disclosed?
- Are commercial relationships transparent?
- Is user data handled responsibly?
- Can readers contact the organization?

For sensitive topics, trustworthiness carries the greatest weight.

## Phase 10: Internal Linking Strategy

Build internal links based on semantic relationships, not arbitrary keyword matching.

Every important page should link to:

- its parent topic,
- relevant supporting articles,
- related products or services,
- comparison pages,
- glossary or definition pages,
- and the appropriate conversion page.

Use descriptive anchors.

Bad anchor:

> Click here

Better anchor:

> Compare private journaling apps

Identify:

- orphan pages,
- weakly linked pages,
- pages receiving too many irrelevant links,
- missing hub pages,
- and opportunities to pass authority to priority pages.

## Phase 12: Content Writing Rules

When writing or rewriting content:

- answer the main query in the first 100 words,
- use plain language,
- keep paragraphs short,
- avoid unnecessary jargon,
- define technical terms,
- use examples,
- acknowledge limitations,
- avoid exaggerated claims,
- use headings that reflect real questions,
- make lists parallel and scannable,
- use tables only when comparison improves understanding,
- and end with a relevant next step.

Do not start with generic phrases such as:

- In today's digital world
- In the fast-paced era
- It is important to note
- Nowadays
- Are you looking for
- Look no further

Avoid repeating the same keyword unnaturally.

Use semantic variations naturally.

## Phase 13: Content Refresh Workflow

For existing pages:

1. Confirm current search intent.
2. Review traffic and query changes.
3. Check whether facts are outdated.
4. Compare current SERP formats.
5. Remove obsolete sections.
6. Add missing questions and entities.
7. Improve the opening answer.
8. Add original insight or evidence.
9. update internal links.
10. update structured data.
11. update publication or review date only when meaningful revisions were made.
12. validate indexing and rendering.

Do not change content merely to create artificial freshness.

# Decision Rules

## When to create a new page

Create a separate page when:

- the query has distinct intent,
- the audience is different,
- the product or use case is meaningfully different,
- the page can provide unique value,
- or combining the topic would make the existing page confusing.

## When to merge pages

Merge when:

- pages target the same intent,
- content is substantially overlapping,
- neither page is strong enough independently,
- or ranking signals are being split.

## When to delete or redirect

Delete or redirect when:

- the page has no useful purpose,
- the information is obsolete,
- the content is duplicated,
- the page has no traffic, links, conversions, or strategic value,
- and no improved replacement is needed.

## When to use FAQ content

Use FAQ content when:

- users genuinely ask the questions,
- answers add value,
- the questions are not already answered clearly,
- and the section supports the page's main intent.

Do not add FAQs only for schema.
